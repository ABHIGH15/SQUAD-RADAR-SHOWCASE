# The Problem

Every hackathon has hundreds of talented builders.

Finding teammates isn't difficult anymore.

Choosing **the right team** is.

Today, participants usually join teams based on random Discord messages, WhatsApp groups, or last-minute conversations, often leading to role conflicts, poor collaboration, and unfinished projects.

The real question isn't:

> **"Who is available?"**

It's:

> **"Should I join this team?"**

SquadRadar exists to answer that question.

---

# Product Vision

SquadRadar is a mobile-first product designed to help hackathon participants make better team decisions through structured, explainable recommendations.

Rather than functioning as another networking platform, SquadRadar focuses on reducing failed team formations by helping builders understand team composition, collaboration risks, and decision confidence before committing to a team.

The long-term vision is to become the decision layer between discovering a team and successfully building together.

---

# Why I Built This

As a student participating in hackathons, I noticed that most existing platforms solve **discovery**, but almost none solve **decision making**.

People don't lose hackathons because they couldn't find teammates.

They lose because they joined the wrong team.

This project is my attempt to rethink that experience from first principles.

# Development Principles

During development, I intentionally followed a product-first engineering philosophy.

Instead of starting with code, the project was built around structured product decisions.

Core principles included:

- Solve the user's decision, not just the workflow.
- Prioritize trust over feature count.
- Build explainable systems instead of black-box behavior.
- Keep business logic independent from UI.
- Design before implementation.
- Validate assumptions through structured reviews.
- Optimize for maintainability and long-term scalability.
